# Interview type: oral exam (graduate admission, program interview)

Applies to interview folders for admission interviews and oral exams. Everything in job.md §1, §2, §5–§8 applies too
(self-introduction frame, must-prepare three, pressure pattern, strengths as one scene, do-not-say list), with the
changes below.

## 1. The examiners only have the application

- Questions start from the submitted statement of purpose, research plan, CV and forms — nothing else
  (grading.md §4). Head each practice question with the paragraph it comes from.
- Follow-ups move one step at a time from that sentence. Research the tutor did shows up only in the second or third
  follow-up.
- The user often forgets what they submitted. Quote the submitted sentence in full when asking about it.

## 2. "Why this program?" comes first

- Research order before answer training (modes/research.md): **① the program (why here) > ② the field basics >
  ③ linking to the submitted plan.** The first practice question is "Why did you apply to this program?"
- The body of that answer is **what this program has**: courses, labs, faculty work, tracks — two items with sources
  from `about/` or `research/`. What the user wants to do is the last line.
- Distinguish "the curriculum includes X" from "X is required or offered every term". State only what the source says.
- The school's name or reputation is never a reason.

## 3. Research plan questions

- Expect: What do you want to study? Why that? How? What do you already know? What if it has been done?
- The plan is an applicant's plan, not a paper. Examiners expect "I know what I want to look at", not a finished
  method. Depth is shown in follow-ups, not by stuffing the document.
- Pushback ("Isn't there research on this already?") > admit > what is still left > what I'd look at
  (grading.md §3).
- Keep the questions the user's research is trying to answer ("Who should decide that?") separate from obvious
  objections. An obvious objection the user raised while reading the draft belongs in the document (modes/writing.md),
  not in practice.

## 4. Background in another field

- Use the career/major switch order from job.md §4.
- Link each earlier experience to one sentence of the plan (experience card Link slot).
- Certificates and grades: say what they are and when, nothing more, unless asked.

## 5. Field basics

- Expect one or two concept questions from the field the plan sits in. Use the Concept frame
  (definition > one example > where it touches my work).
- If the user does not understand a concept in the plan, that is research first, not answer practice.
- Current topics in the field: modes/research.md §3. Only bring up a topic when it links to the plan.

## 6. Self-introduction for admissions

Same frame as job.md §2, with [Here] = what the user wants to study in this program, and [Now] including the field
or job they come from. When no self-introduction was submitted, keep it consistent with the statement of purpose.

## 7. Before the exam

- Confirm format, time, number of examiners and whether slides or notes are allowed — from the official notice only
  (honesty.md §5). Unknown > a task to ask the office.
- Prepare the closing remark and two questions to ask (about the program or the lab, not the admissions result).

## 8. Region notes

- Some programs run the interview as a short exam with a set question; others as a conversation with faculty.
  Record the expected format in `directions.md`.
- A meeting with a faculty member before applying is a separate stage; its notes are private material and never the
  starting point of a practice question (grading.md §4).
